The problem

Cold-calling a business about its website only works if you can point to something real: the site is slow on phones, its SSL certificate expires in 12 days, it has no DMARC record so its emails land in spam, or AI assistants never recommend it.

Finding that by hand takes 30 to 60 minutes per business. I wanted it in under a minute, with every claim marked as either verified or inferred, so nothing I say on a call is a guess.

What it does

Lead discovery 6

  • harvest finds businesses in a niche with no website
  • registry imports from government open-data portals
  • geo_browse drills State → County → City
  • batch scans lists overnight and ranks them
  • contacts finds who to call, from the site itself
  • calltime best time to call from the area code

Tech & security 11

  • fingerprint + wappalyzer detect CMS, plugins, stack
  • headers grades CSP, HSTS and friends
  • ssl_audit + ssl_deep certificate and TLS via sslyze
  • dns_audit SPF, DMARC, DKIM, CAA, DNSSEC
  • subdomains via Certificate Transparency
  • nvd known-vulnerability lookup

Speed, SEO & access 8

  • performance Google PageSpeed Insights
  • accessibility WCAG checks with axe-core
  • seo + aeo search and AI-search readiness
  • crawl + pageaudit beyond the homepage
  • wayback site history from archive.org
  • screenshots desktop + phone renders

AI & sales output 7

  • ai_visibility asks an AI who it recommends locally
  • pitch_ai writes a call pitch with Gemini
  • dossier picks the few problems worth raising
  • compare puts a prospect next to rivals
  • fixpack ready-to-paste structured data
  • report branded PDF with ReportLab

Architecture

Next.js 15 dashboard (React 19, TypeScript) │ scan bar · X-ray · call brief · leads · harvest queue · analytics ▼ FastAPI service (35 endpoints) ──► stateless analyzer: URL in → ScanResult out │ ├─ httpx fetch (polite, passive) │ ├─ Playwright + Chromium render │ ├─ sslyze · dnspython · RDAP · crt.sh │ └─ PageSpeed · NVD · Gemini · archive.org ▼ SQLite store ── scan history · lead tracking · backups · CSV export

Every module writes into one shared ScanResult schema. That single contract is what stopped a 36-module codebase from sprawling: new checks plug in without touching the dashboard.

Principles I held to

  • Passive only. It reads what a site serves on a normal visit. No path brute-forcing, no probing forms, nothing a firewall would flag as an attack.
  • Verified vs inferred. Every finding carries a confidence flag. Only things the server stated directly (a header, a certificate field) are marked safe to quote.
  • No scare tactics. Exposed versions are reported as facts; the tool never claims a site “is hacked”. Wrong security claims in writing are a legal risk, not a sales tactic.
  • Runs on a laptop. One double-click starts the API and dashboard. No servers, no monthly bill.
